Ming Li

NHMRC Emerging Leadership Fellow and Associate Professor at UNSW Sydney.

Ming leads research in microfluidics, nanomedicine, biosensors, MEMS, and lab-on-a-chip technologies. She received her PhD from the University of Wollongong and held postdoctoral appointments at the University of Houston and UCLA before joining Macquarie University and later UNSW Sydney.

Research focus

My research focuses on developing a droplet digital microfluidic platform for CRISPR-based nucleic acid detection. The project integrates microfluidic device design and fabrication with CRISPR/Cas techniques for sensitive, quantitative, and multiplexed detection of nucleic acid biomarkers at the single-molecule level, with particular emphasis on double-emulsion droplet generation and manipulation for molecular diagnostics and early disease detection.

Research focus

My proposed research project focuses on droplet-microfluidically fabricated magnetic microrobots for controlled cargo transport, magnetic targeting, and localized release. The project investigates how microrobot structure and magnetic configuration influence transport and delivery in confined, branched, and flowing biomimetic microenvironments.

Research focus

My research focuses on electrochemical aptamer-based biosensors for continuous therapeutic drug monitoring. I am particularly interested in combining nanostructured conductive materials, antifouling interfaces, and probe engineering to improve sensor sensitivity, stability, and performance in complex biological samples.
